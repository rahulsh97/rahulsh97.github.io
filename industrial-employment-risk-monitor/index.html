The economic question

A warning system, not a crystal ball

The monitor estimates whether a country–industry observation resembles earlier cases that were followed by a fall of at least 20% in employees over three years.

Illustrative case · Albania · Basic metals · 2023

Calibrated risk25%
TierHigh

This means roughly 25 cases in 100 with a similar statistical profile would be expected to experience a severe contraction. It does not mean that employment will definitely fall.

The live monitor lets the user replace this example with any scoreable country–industry pair.

What the interface explains

  1. Signal. The calibrated risk and the correct historical comparison.
  2. Evidence. The statistical warning signals behind the classification.
  3. Next investigation. Questions on demand, firm exits, technology, trade and worker transitions.

Why it is credible

Performance and limitations stay visible

The model is trained on earlier years and evaluated on later years it had not seen. The interface reports uncertainty and false-warning trade-offs rather than presenting scores as certainty.

Out-of-time testingROC-AUC 0.683; PR-AUC 0.203 against an 0.108 base rate.
Calibrated tiersObserved contraction rates rise from about 5 in 100 in Low to 28 in 100 in High.
Missing remains missingCases without sufficient indicators are labelled “insufficient data”; they are never converted to zero.
No causal claimWarning signals are associations and should trigger investigation, not automatic intervention.
Comparable specificationThe outcome uses employees consistently and excludes nominal monetary levels.
Known weaknessPerformance is weaker for economies absent from model training and is flagged at the point of use.
