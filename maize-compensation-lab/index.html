Who should be compensated
when maize prices rise?

A maize price rise hurts households that buy more maize than they sell, and the poorest are mostly buyers. Compare how six ways of spending the same compensation budget protect them, under different price scenarios and policy objectives, and see how much the answer depends on what the government knows.

What this is. A viewer for precomputed results. Every number on this page was produced by a validated analysis of the Malawi Sixth Integrated Household Survey (IHS6, 2024/25; 12,502 households, 782 enumeration areas) and stored as aggregate tables. Nothing is simulated, re-estimated or interpolated in the browser. Before any simulation, the analysis reproduced all 103 published IHS6 poverty figures exactly, including every district.

Welfare model. First-order money-metric effect of a price change: maize sales × farm-gate price change − purchases × retail price change, allowing the two prices to move differently. Maize a household grows and eats has no first-order effect. An optional second-order term allows consumers to substitute. Values are real 2024 kwacha per person, against the official poverty line.

Rules and budget. Every rule spends exactly the same budget, set as a share of the aggregate losses of losing households. PMT and exposure rules use household demographics, dwelling, assets and land. They are cross-fitted by enumeration area, so no household is scored by a model trained on its own area. Coverage equals the government's survey estimate of the post-shock poverty rate.

Uncertainty. A Rao–Wu complex-survey bootstrap (200 replicates; enumeration areas resampled within strata). In each replicate the government's targeting models and estimates are re-fitted and outcomes re-evaluated. It covers three scenarios. The 972-scenario grid is a deterministic sensitivity analysis, not a probability distribution.

Limitations.

  • Scenarios, not forecasts or causal estimates: no wage, supply or general-equilibrium effects.
  • PMT and exposure rules are an upper bound. They are fitted on the same survey they are judged on; real registries are older and noisier.
  • Purchases are 7-day recall, annualised, and fieldwork coincided with a maize price spike. Purchases are deflated with general rather than maize-specific prices.
  • Geography is district level only.
  • The bootstrap excludes weight re-calibration, finite-population corrections and measurement error.

Sources and provenance. National Statistical Office of Malawi, IHS6 2024/25 (World Bank Microdata Library, MWI_2024-2025_IHS-VI_v01_M). Household microdata are not published here and are not redistributable. Displayed values come from validated run maize_20261025T173130_1dafdd: its aggregate tables and manifest are in data/, and the build checks every table against the manifest's SHA-256 hashes.
